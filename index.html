T-JOINT WELD SIM

Weld an 8″ coupon or a pipe joint in any of the AWS test positions: a ¼″ fillet on a T-joint (1F–4F) or a multi-pass ⅜″ V-groove on backing (1G–4G), or an open-root pipe joint (1G rolled, 2G, 5G, 6G), with MIG (GMAW), stick (SMAW), or TIG (GTAW). Or gas weld 14 ga sheet with oxy-acetylene (OAW): butt, outside corner, fillet and lap joints. Steer the torch with your mouse and keyboard, then cut and etch your weld to see how you did.

How to weld it

  • Pull the trigger (LMB or Space) at the START soapstone mark and travel left → right.
  • Moving the mouse sideways is your travel speed. Aim for a smooth 9–13 ipm. That's slow and steady.
  • Keep the cursor on the joint line for a ½″ contact-tip-to-work distance. Raise it for longer stickout, lower it to get closer.
  • Hold a 45° work angle (A/D) and a 5–15° travel angle. W tilts toward push, S toward drag. Hold Shift for fine moves.
  • Run past the STOP mark, release, and press Enter to inspect.
  • Hold LMB/Space to press the foot pedal (arc on). Q/E or the mouse wheel set how far down the pedal is (amps).
  • The mouse sets travel (sideways) and arc length (up/down). Hold it tight, about the tungsten diameter (3/32″), and never touch the tungsten to the puddle.
  • Wait for a wet, shiny puddle (watch the heat gauge), then dab filler with F: dab, move, dab. That rhythm makes the "stacked dimes." Travel about 4–6 ipm.
  • Push the torch 5–20° (W/S) at a 45° work angle (A/D).
  • At the end, ease off the pedal to fill the crater before you let go, or it will crack.
  • Dipped the tungsten? Let off the pedal and press G to regrind it.
  • Hold LMB/Space to bring the flame onto the work. The mouse sets travel (sideways) and how far the inner cone is from the metal (up/down). Hold it 1/16–1/8″ off. Touch the puddle and the torch pops.
  • A flame heats slowly: hold still until a shiny, wet puddle forms (watch the heat gauge), then start moving at about 4–6 ipm.
  • Point the flame ahead at 35–55° (W/S) and dab the rod into the front edge of the puddle with F, never into the flame. Outside corner: no rod, just melt the edges together.
  • Too hot burns a hole in 14 ga. Lift the flame or speed up when the puddle gets big.
  • Bring the rod tip to the START mark, then click and hold (LMB/Space) to strike. Holding keeps the arc lit; letting go breaks it.
  • The rod burns off as you weld (about 9–10″ a minute). Keep lowering the cursor to feed it in and hold a tight arc, about ⅛″ (the core-wire diameter).
  • Too close and the rod sticks. Too long and the arc spatters, causes porosity, and goes out.
  • Drag the rod 5–15° (S/W; never push) at a 45° work angle (A/D). Travel about 6–9 ipm.
  • When the rod burns down to a stub, press R for a fresh one, move the cursor back up, and restrike just ahead of the crater.
  • Finish with Enter, then chip the slag before inspection.
